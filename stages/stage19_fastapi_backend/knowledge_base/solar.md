# Solar Power

Solar power converts sunlight into electricity, mainly through photovoltaic
(PV) panels made of silicon solar cells. When light hits a cell, it knocks
electrons loose and creates a flow of current.

Panels are most efficient when they face the sun directly, so many
installations use tilted, fixed mounts or motorized trackers that follow the
sun through the day. Efficiency also drops in high heat, which is why some
utility-scale farms are built in cooler, high-altitude desert regions rather
than the hottest lowland deserts.

Because panels only generate power while the sun is up, solar installations
are often paired with battery storage (commonly lithium-ion) so homes and
grids can draw power after sunset. A home rooftop system typically ranges
from 5 to 10 kilowatts, while utility-scale solar farms can produce hundreds
of megawatts.

The main advantages of solar are low operating cost once installed and
minimal moving parts, which keeps maintenance simple. The main drawback is
intermittency: output depends on weather and daylight hours.
